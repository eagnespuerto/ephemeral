How to use Ephemeral

  1. Search by name: type a name or designation (Apophis, 2024 YR4, 1P, C/2023 A3, Mars) and press Enter. Ephemeral asks JPL Horizons, the MPC Ephemeris Service and JPL Scout, and fills the card with the best ephemeris. Click Use to switch source. Set the span and step under the name. Horizons and MPC go through Ephemeral's proxy (see the sidebar).
  2. Or paste an ephemeris into an object card. It reads JPL Horizons observer tables (text or CSV), the MPC Ephemeris Service, Find_Orb, MPC 80-column astrometry, and simple time RA Dec [Δ] tables. Sexagesimal RA is hours; decimal RA is degrees. Positions are taken as J2000.
  3. Discovery POV shows the sky from the discovery site. Type its MPC code in the card. Horizons topocentric tables and 80-column astrometry (the * line) fill it in for you.
  4. Your POV uses your location. Click Use my location or type a code.
  5. In the domes the track is drawn against the sky at the chosen time, like a finder path among the stars. The dot is where the object is at that moment.
  6. Sky chart is a zoomable RA/Dec plot. When the ephemeris has a distance (Δ), the dashed tracks show parallax from each site.
  7. Space View turns RA, Dec and Δ into a heliocentric arc. If the object has a name, Ping Scout asks JPL Scout for its tentative orbit and draws the sample orbits as a cloud.
  8. Share link packs everything into the URL.

Astronomy: Astronomy Engine. Stars and constellation lines: d3-celestial (BSD-3). Observatory codes: Minor Planet Center. Tentative orbits: NASA/JPL CNEOS Scout. Not for planning critical observations.
